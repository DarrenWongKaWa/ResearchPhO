\problem{1}{A two-cycle quantum conveyor: bands, projectors and flavors}{10.0}

In a \emph{Thouless pump} the parameters of a lattice Hamiltonian are changed slowly and periodically in time. After each period $T$ the Hamiltonian is the same as before, yet particles can be carried along the lattice. For non-interacting fermions that completely fill a band separated by gaps from the other bands, the number of particles carried past any bond in one period is an integer, the Chern number of the band.\footnote{This problem is based on M.~J\"urgensen, J.~Steiner, G.~Refael and M.~C.~Rechtsman, arXiv:2504.09338, and S.-M.~Huang, arXiv:2608.06777.}

This problem studies spinless fermions on a chain with five sites per unit cell (Fig.~\ref{fig:system}). There are $N$ cells, $5N$ sites and $N$ particles, one particle per cell on average. The Hamiltonian of this ``multi-band fractional pump'' (MFP) is
\[
\begin{split}
H_{\rm MFP}(t)&=-\sum_j\big[J_j(t)c_{j+1}^\dagger c_j+\hc\big]
+\sum_{j}\sum_{r=1}^{3}U_r n_jn_{j+r},\\
J_j(t)&=J+K\cos\left(\frac{2\pi t}{T}+\frac{4\pi j}{5}\right),\qquad n_j=c_j^\dagger c_j ,
\end{split}
\]
where $c_j$ removes a fermion from site $j$ and $U_r>0$ is the repulsion between two particles $r$ sites apart. The ideal motion studied here is to the left; the target transport is $Q_*=-1/2$ particle per period.

Without interactions, translation by one cell lets one write the one-particle problem as a $5\times5$ matrix $h(k,\phi)$ for each quasi-momentum $k$; its five eigenvalues are the bands (Fig.~\ref{fig:bands}). From all the Bloch states of one band, or of a group of bands, one can build orbitals that are localized in space, one per cell for each band (\emph{Wannier orbitals}). The expectation value of the position in such an orbital is its \emph{centre}. If every orbital of a set is occupied and every centre moves by $\Delta x$, the transported charge per cell is $\Delta x/a$, where $a$ is the cell length. Orbitals built from band 1 alone move by $+2$ cells per period (Fig.~\ref{fig:conveyor}a). Mixing bands 1 and 2 gives two localized orbitals per cell, called flavor $A$ and flavor $B$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:conveyor}b). A strong finite-range repulsion can make the particles occupy one flavor only.

The papers behind this problem ask two questions. Can repulsion turn this integer pump into one that carries a fraction of a particle per period, although there is one particle per cell? And how can a moving group of bands be described without choosing a basis inside it (a ``projector'' description)?

\smallheading{Useful information}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=6mm,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
\item An orthogonal projector obeys $p^2=p=p^\dagger$; its rank is $\Tr p$.
\item On a ring of length $L$, let $P$ project onto the occupied one-particle states of a group of bands. For a long ring, the eigenvalues of $Pe^{2\pi ix/L}P$ on the range of $P$ are close to $e^{2\pi ix_\alpha/L}$, where $x_\alpha$ are the centres of the maximally localized orbitals of the group. In momentum space this operator is the \emph{Wilson loop}: the ordered product of the overlaps of the band eigenvectors around the Brillouin zone at fixed $\phi$.
\item For a unit vector $\bm n$ and real $x$: $e^{ix\bm n\cdot\bm\sigma}=\id\cos x+i(\bm n\cdot\bm\sigma)\sin x$, where $\id$ is the identity.
\end{itemize}

\parttitle{A}{Fractional transport}{1.5}
Suppose that the occupied orbitals follow one flavor exactly. An $N$-cell ring has cell length $a$ and contains $N$ particles. After $2T$, each occupied orbital has an unwrapped centre displacement $\Delta x_j=-a$. Define the spatially averaged transported particle number by
\[
Q(0,2T)=\frac{1}{Na}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\Delta x_j.
\]
The single-period values $Q(0,T)$ and $Q(T,2T)$ are defined in the same way from the displacements during each period.

\parttitle{B}{From the lattice to a projector}{4.1}
Write the site index as $j=5R+s$, with cell $R$ and orbital $s=0,\ldots,4$. Define
\[
t_s(\phi)=J+K\cos(\phi+4\pi s/5),\qquad
c_{R,s}=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_k e^{ikR}c_{k,s},
\]
where $k=2\pi m/N$ is a dimensionless cell momentum. The single-particle part of $H_{\rm MFP}$ is
\[
H_0=-\sum_R\left[\sum_{s=0}^{3}t_s c_{R,s+1}^\dagger c_{R,s}

\problem{2}{Correlations and the physical current}{10.0}

This problem uses the driven chain of Problem 1 but can be solved on its own. Spinless fermions hop on a chain with five sites per cell, one particle per cell on average; the hoppings are modulated with period $T$ and particles $r=1,2,3$ sites apart repel each other:
\[
H_{\rm MFP}(t)=-\sum_j\big[J_j(t)c_{j+1}^\dagger c_j+\hc\big]+\sum_{j}\sum_{r=1}^{3}U_r n_jn_{j+r}.
\]
In the ideal ``fractional'' regime every particle occupies one of two localized orbital types (flavors $A$ or $B$) and follows it, which transports $-1/2$ particle per period. In a long chain, regions of the other flavor (\emph{domains}) can appear. A practical description therefore starts from a simple \emph{reference state}, transforms the Hamiltonian to frames in which that reference is simple, keeps part of the transformed generator, and bounds the rest. This problem examines that strategy with exactly solvable models and exact identities. Its question is: when a reference state is dressed by correlations and followed in a moving frame, which steps are exact, where does the first approximation enter, and how is the physical current obtained?

\smallheading{Useful information}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=6mm,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
\item If $i\partial_t\ket\psi=H\ket\psi$ and $\ket\xi=U^\dagger\ket\psi$ with a differentiable unitary $U(t)$, then $i\partial_t\ket\xi=(U^\dagger HU-iU^\dagger\dot U)\ket\xi$.
\item If $i\partial_t\ket\xi=\mathcal K\ket\xi$ and $O(t)$ is an operator, then $\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\bra\xi O\ket\xi=\bra\xi\big(\partial_tO+i[\mathcal K,O]\big)\ket\xi$.
\item Pauli matrices: $\sigma_a\sigma_b=\delta_{ab}\id+i\epsilon_{abc}\sigma_c$, e.g.\ $[\sigma_z,\sigma_x]=2i\sigma_y$; operators on different spins commute. $\id$ is the identity.
\item A \emph{Slater determinant} is a state of independent fermions in chosen orbitals; a single (double) excitation replaces one (two) of its occupied orbitals.
\end{itemize}

\problem{3}{How long can the conveyor operate?}{10.0}

The ideal fractional pump of Problems 1 and 2 transports $Q_*=-1/2$ particle per drive period $T$ (transport to the right is positive). In a real, large, interacting chain the transported charge slowly drifts away from $Q_*$. This problem builds a method that certifies, window by window, that the charge stays within a tolerance $\delta$. Its question is the open question of the first paper: for how long can one guarantee that the transported charge stays within $\delta$ of $Q_*$, and what would be needed to predict when it fails? It can be solved on its own.

Throughout, $\rho'$ denotes the exact state written in some moving frame, $\sigma'$ a simpler reference state in the same frame, and $I'$ the physical current operator written in that frame. A region $X$ is a group of cells around the bond where the current is measured (Fig.~\ref{fig:local}); $\bar X$ is the rest of the chain.

\smallheading{Useful information}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=6mm,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
\item $\norm A$ is the operator norm and $\norm A_1$ the trace norm (sum of singular values); $|\Tr(AB)|\leq\norm A_1\norm B$. For density matrices, $\norm{\rho-\sigma}_1\leq2$ and $\norm{\rho-\ket a\bra a}_1\leq2\sqrt{1-\bra a\rho\ket a}$.
\item The reduced state on $X$ is $\rho_X=\Tr_{\bar X}\rho$ (trace over $\bar X$ only). If $O_X$ acts only on $X$, then $\Tr(\rho O_X)=\Tr_X(\rho_XO_X)$ and $\Tr_{\bar X}(O_X\rho)=O_X\rho_X$. For $B_{\bar X}$ acting only on $\bar X$, $\Tr_{\bar X}(B_{\bar X}A)=\Tr_{\bar X}(AB_{\bar X})$.
\item For commuting projectors, $1-\prod_{x\in X}(1-q_x)\leq\sum_{x\in X}q_x$.
\end{itemize}

\parttitle{A}{Local accuracy in a large system}{5.3}
Consider the product state
\[
\ket{\psi_N}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^N
\big[\sqrt{1-p}\ket A_j+\sqrt p\ket B_j\big],
\qquad0<p<1,\quad\braket A B=0,
\]
with $p$ independent of $N$, and use the fidelity $\mathcal F(\rho,\ket a)=\bra a\rho\ket a$.

\question{A.1}{1.1}{Find its fidelity with $\ket A^{\otimes N}$. Then find the fidelity of an $m$-cell subsystem with $\ket A^{\otimes m}$, with $m$ fixed as $N$ increases. What does this comparison imply about using global fidelity to diagnose local transport?}

Let $\rho'$ and $\sigma'$ be normalized density matrices in the same moving frame. Let $I_X$ be supported in $X$, and suppose $\norm{I'-I_X}\leq\eta_X$. Reduced density matrices on $X$ are denoted by $\rho'_X$ and $\sigma'_X$. Set $\Delta I=\Tr[(\rho'-\sigma')I']$.

\question{A.2}{2.2}{Prove
$|\Delta I|\leq\norm{I_X}\norm{\rho'_X-\sigma'_X}_1+2\eta_X$.
If the reference is a product state in this frame, define
$q_x=1-\ket{A_{0,x}}\bra{A_{0,x}}$ and
$D_X=\sum_{x\in X}\langle q_x\rangle_{\rho'}$.
Deduce
$|\Delta I|\leq2\norm{I_X}\min(1,\sqrt{D_X})+2\eta_X$.
State which form remains valid for a general correlated reference.}

Now let the two states evolve as
\[
i\dot\rho'=[\mathcal K_{\rm corr},\rho'],\qquad
i\dot\sigma'=[\mathcal K_{\rm ret},\sigma'],\qquad
\mathcal K_{\rm corr}=\mathcal K_{\rm ret}+\mathcal R,
\]
where $\mathcal K_{\rm corr}$ is the exact generator in the chosen frame and $\mathcal K_{\rm ret}$ the part kept in the reference description. Both states are normalized; their initial values need not coincide. Write
\[
\mathcal K_{\rm ret}=\mathcal K_X+\mathcal K_{\bar X}+\mathcal K_{\partial X},\qquad
\sigma'_X=\Tr_{\bar X}\sigma',
\]
where the first two terms act only inside and only outside $X$, and the third contains the couplings between both regions. (The ``composite-band gap'' is the single-particle gap between bands 2 and 3 of the chain, Problem 1.)

\question{A.3}{2.0}{\textbf{(a)} Derive the equation for $\sigma'_X$. Show why the contribution from $\mathcal K_{\bar X}$ vanishes under the partial trace.\par
\textbf{(b)} Is $\sigma'_X(0)$ and $\mathcal K_X(t)$ enough to solve that equation? Identify the additional object required by the boundary term. Does the composite-band gap supply it?\par
\textbf{(c)} Let $\delta\rho=\rho'-\sigma'$. Derive its exact equation and identify the initial-mismatch and omitted-dynamics inputs. Explain why rewriting this equation is not yet a bound on $\norm{\delta\rho_X(t)}_1$.}

\parttitle{B}{A conditional operating-time certificate}{1.6}
Let $\alpha,I_0>0$ and $\eta,\delta_0\geq0$. Assume that, for every $0\leq s\leq t_{\max}$,
\[
D_X(s)\leq\alpha^2s^2,\qquad
\norm{I_X(s)}\leq I_0,\qquad\eta_X(s)\leq\eta ,
\]
and that every relevant two-period reference window satisfies $|\Qbar_{\rm ref}(t)-Q_*|\leq\delta_0$. Here $\alpha$, $I_0$ and $\eta$ have units of inverse time; $\delta_0$ and charge are dimensionless. Define
\[
\Qbar(t)=\frac12\int_{t-2T}^{t}\langle I(s)\rangle\,\dd s,
\qquad2T\leq t\leq t_{\max},
\]
and $\Qbar_{\rm ref}(t)$ in the same way with the reference state. The factor is $1/2$, not $1/(2T)$: this is charge per drive period averaged over two periods.
