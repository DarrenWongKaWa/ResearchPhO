{\large ResearchPhO --- Solutions and marking scheme \hfill \textbf{30 points}}

\smallheading{How to use this booklet}
Each answer is followed by its marking scheme; the numbers in a marking line add up to the points of the question. (M) marks a modelling step: setting up the model, choosing the physics, interpreting a result, judging its scope. (C) marks a computation step: algebra, solving, evaluating. Most of each problem's marks go to (M). Appendices A--D contain the research context; Appendix E, the toolbox, gives the smallest worked example of each tool from ``Useful information''.

\pagetitle{Appendix E. Toolbox: the smallest worked examples}
One entry for every tool stated under ``Useful information'' (or in a hint or a given identity) that is above the reader's level. Each entry takes the smallest case in which the tool does something: a 3-site ring before the 5-site one; the $N=2$ case of a product identity before general $N$. The example teaches the tool, not an exam step.
